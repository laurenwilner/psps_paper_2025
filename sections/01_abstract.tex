\textbf{Abstract}


\textit{Rationale.}
To prevent wildfires, utilities may pre-emptively de-energize power lines (public safety power shutoffs [PSPS]).\citep{pse_healthy_energy} These PSPS events may unintentionally impact health, and these effects may be more serious if a wildfire still occurs.

\textit{Objectives.} 
To assess the effect of PSPS events, wildfire fine particulate matter (\PM) exposure, and their co-occurrence on cause-specific healthcare utilization.

\textit{Methods.} 
We used circuit-level utility data\citep{cpuc_psps_reports} to identify PSPS events, and then categorized their magnitude as mild, moderate, or severe based on the number of people without power. We determined zip code-level exposure in California from 2013--2019. We leveraged zip code-level average daily wildfire \PM estimates\citep{aguilera2023novel} and respiratory, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)-specific, cardiovascular, and psychiatric hospital and emergency department encounters from the California Department of Health Care Access and Information.\citep{hcai_datasets} Using a time-stratified case-crossover design, we estimated the impact of PSPS events alone, wildfire \PM alone, and their co-exposure on healthcare utilization.

\textit{Measurements and main results.}
Severe PSPS events were associated with elevated odds of a same-day respiratory encounter (relative: OR = \resphybor{}, 95\% confidence interval [CI]: \resphybcilow{}, \resphybcihigh{}; absolute: OR = \respabsor{}, 95\% CI: \respabscilow{}, \respabscihigh{}). These associations were stronger for COPD-specific encounters. Compared to no PSPS exposure, co-exposure was associated with higher odds of a respiratory encounter(relative: \respcombhybperc\% [95\% CI: \respcombhyblow{}, \respcombhybhigh{}]; absolute: \respcombabsperc\% [95\% CI: \respcombabslow{}, \respcombabshigh{}]), with evidence of a multiplicative interaction. All associations were null for cardiovascular and psychiatric encounters.  

\textit{Conclusions.}
PSPS events aim to prevent wildfires but often co-occur with smoke events, creating a dual hazard. Severe PSPS events may increase risk of respiratory healthcare utilization, especially when they co-occur with wildfire \PM.
