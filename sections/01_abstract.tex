\textbf{Abstract}


\textit{Rationale.}
To prevent wildfires, electric utilities may preemptively de-energize power lines (public safety power shutoffs [PSPS]).\citep{pse_healthy_energy} These PSPS events may unintentionally impact health, with potentially worse impacts if a wildfire still occurs.

\textit{Objectives.} 
To assess the effect of PSPS events, wildfire fine particulate matter (\PM) exposure, and their co-occurrence on cause-specific healthcare utilization.

\textit{Methods.} 
We used circuit-level utility data\citep{cpuc_psps_reports} to identify PSPS events in California, and then categorized their magnitude as mild, moderate, or severe based on the number of people without power. We determined zip code-level exposure from 2013--2019. We leveraged zip code-level average daily wildfire \PM estimates\citep{aguilera2023novel} and all respiratory, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)-specific, cardiovascular, and psychiatric emergency department visits and hospital admissions among adults aged 20+ from the California Department of Health Care Access and Information.\citep{hcai_datasets} Using a time-stratified case-crossover design, we estimated the impact of PSPS events alone, wildfire \PM alone, and their co-occurrence on healthcare utilization.

\textit{Measurements and main results.}
Severe PSPS events were associated with elevated odds of same-day respiratory encounters (OR = \respabsor{}, 95\% CI: \respabscilow{}, \respabscihigh{}). These associations were stronger for COPD-specific encounters. Compared to no PSPS or wildfire exposure, joint PSPS and \SI[per-mode=symbol]{10}{\micro\gram\per\cubic\metre} higher wildfire \PM was associated with higher odds of respiratory encounters (\respcombabsperc\% [95\% CI: \respcombabslow{}, \respcombabshigh{}]), with evidence of multiplicative interaction. Associations were null for cardiovascular and psychiatric encounters.  

\textit{Conclusions.}
 PSPS events may increase risk of respiratory healthcare utilization, especially when they co-occur with wildfire \PM.
