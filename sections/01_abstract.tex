\textbf{Abstract}

To prevent wildfires, electric utilities may preemptively de-energize power lines (public safety power shutoffs [PSPS]).\citep{PSEHealthyEnergy_PSPS} These PSPS events may unintentionally harm health, especially if a wildfire occurs despite the power shutoff, and among those with pre-existing conditions like chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) or who rely on electricity-dependent medical equipment. Here, we assessed the effect of PSPS events, wildfire fine particulate matter (\PM) exposure, and their co-occurrence on cause-specific healthcare utilization. We identified PSPS events in California from 2013--2019,\citep{cpuc_psps_reports} categorizing their magnitude based on the number of people without power by zip code. We leveraged zip code-level average daily wildfire \PM estimates\citep{aguilera2023novel} and respiratory, COPD-specific, cardiovascular, and psychiatric emergency department visits and unscheduled hospital admissions among adults aged 20+ from the California Department of Health Care Access and Information.\citep{hcai_datasets} Using a time-stratified case-crossover design, we estimated the impact of same-day and 3-day lagged (lag 0-3) PSPS events alone, wildfire \PM alone, and their co-occurrence on healthcare utilization. Severe PSPS events were associated with elevated odds of same-day respiratory encounters (OR = \respabsorSameDay{}, 95\% CI: \respabscilowSameDay{}, \respabscihighSameDay{}). These associations were stronger for COPD and similar for lag 0-3. Compared to no PSPS or wildfire exposure, joint PSPS and \SI[per-mode=symbol]{10}{\micro\gram\per\cubic\metre} higher wildfire \PM was associated with higher odds of respiratory encounters (\respcombabspercSameDay\% [95\% CI: \respcombabslowSameDay{}, \respcombabshighSameDay{}]), with evidence of multiplicative interaction. Associations were null for cardiovascular and psychiatric encounters. PSPS events, especially when co-occurring with wildfire \PM, may increase risk of acute respiratory care utilization.
