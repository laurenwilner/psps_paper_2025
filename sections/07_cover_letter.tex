Please find attached the manuscript: "Co-exposure to planned power outages and wildfire \PM and acute healthcare utilization in California," which we are submitting for consideration as an Original Research Article in the American Journal of Respiratory, Critical Care, and Pulmonary Medicine. This work has not been previously published in whole or in part, and there are no closely related articles currently under review. All authors have reviewed the manuscript prior to submission, and no authors declare a conflict of interest.

Wildfires and public safety power shutoffs (PSPS) are increasingly common events that may have significant implications for public health, particularly respiratory health. Our study leverages circuit-level utility data and zip code-level emergency department and hospital encounter data to examine the impact of PSPS events and wildfire \PM, both alone and together, on healthcare utilization. Key findings include:

\begin{itemize}
\item Severe PSPS events were associated with elevated same-day respiratory emergency department visits and hospital admissions
\item Joint exposure to PSPS and wildfire particulate matter showed multiplicative interactions in respiratory healthcare utilization
\item Particularly strong associations were observed for COPD-specific emergency department visits and hospital admissions
\end{itemize}

We anticipate that these results will be of high interest to your readers, given the growing concern about worsening climate change-related environmental exposures and their potential health impacts. Our findings are critical to better understanding the respiratory health risks associated with power outages and wildfire \PM exposure. Further, these results will have important implications for public health preparedness and disaster mitigation strategies, particularly for respiratory and critical care practitioners.
