Please find attached the manuscript: "Co-exposure to planned power outages and wildfire \PM and acute healthcare utilization in California," which we are submitting for consideration as an Original Research Article in the American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine. This work has not been previously published in whole or in part, and there are no closely related articles currently under review. All authors have reviewed the manuscript prior to submission, and no authors declare a conflict of interest.

Wildfires and public safety power shutoffs (PSPS) are increasingly common events that may have significant implications for population health. We evaluate their independent and joint impacts on zip code-level emergency department and hospital encounters in California from 2013 -- 2019. Key findings include:

\begin{itemize}
\item Severe PSPS events were associated with elevated same-day all-cause respiratory and COPD emergency department visits and hospital admissions
\item Joint exposure to severe PSPS events and wildfire fine particulate matter showed multiplicative interactions on respiratory healthcare utilization
\item For PSPS events, we saw particularly strong associations for COPD-specific emergency department visits and hospital admissions
\end{itemize}

We anticipate that these results will be of high interest to your readers, given the growing concern about worsening climate change-related environmental exposures and their potential health impacts. Our findings are critical to better understanding the respiratory health risks associated with power outages and wildfire \PM exposure. Further, these results will have important implications for public health preparedness and disaster mitigation strategies, particularly for respiratory and critical care practitioners.
