\section{Methods}
\subsection{Study population}
In this zip code-level time-stratified case-crossover study in California, we used data on hospital admissions and emergency department visits among adults $\geq$ 20 years of age from 2013 -- 2019 from the California Department of Health Care Access and Information (HCAI) .\citep{hcai_datasets} Our study included XXX patients aged XX-XX years, living in XXX zip codes across California (Figure X: map of zips included). Based on prior power outage and wildfire literature, we studied respiratory, cardiovascular, and psychiatric health endpoints.\citep{casey2020power, casey2024measuring}

\subsection{Exposures and covariates}

\subsubsection{PSPS data}
We measured exposure to PSPS events from 2013--2022 using data from PSE Healthy Energy, which compiled PSPS events from the three major energy providers in California, Pacific Gas and Electric (PG\&E), San Diego Gas and Electric (SDG\&E), and Southern California Edison (SCE).\citep{cpuc_psps_reports} 

Our PSPS dataset contains information at the circuit level on every PSPS event that affected PG\&E, SDG\&E or SCE. A circuit is defined as XXXX. A PSPS event may impact multiple circuits, so each circuit-event had a start time, end time, duration, and number of residential and commercial customers impacted.\citep{pse_healthy_energy} Despite circuits being energized and de-energized throughout events, we lacked information on this, and therefore we assumed that the entire circuit was without power for the duration of a PSPS event affecting that circuit. 

The circuits were provided as geospatial polygons with a XX km buffer around the circuit polyline that was approximated to include all customers served by that circuit. We overlaid these circuits with high-resolution gridded estimates of population from the 2020 census in California\citep{depsky2022high} to estimate the number of people affected by each outage along the circuit. In the absence of more detailed information on customer distribution, we assumed an even distribution of customers across the circuit. We performed a second spatial overlay of these circuits with zip codes to determine the area of each circuit that overlapped with each zip code. Accordingly, we used the proportion of the area intersecting a given zip code as a weight and calculated the number of customers for every circuit-zip code combination. Finally, we summed customer-circuit-zip code counts to estimate the number of customers impacted by zip code.

We included two daily-zip code exposure metrics in these datasets meant to capture different aspects of PSPS events. The first metric was the total number of customers impacted in each zip code. Because of highly variable population counts within zip codes, we also constructed a second relative metric meant to measure the severity of the PSPS event. The second metric was computed by multiplying the total number of customers out by the proportion of customers out in each zip code. Exposure for both metrics was categorized as no exposure or mild, moderate, or severe exposure based on tertiles of the non-zero exposure values of each metric for each zip code-day.

\subsubsection{Wildfire smoke data}
We used daily wildfire \PM data at the zip code level.\citep{aguilera2023novel} We averaged wildfire \PM exposure across the 4 days leading up to and the day of interest (either a case or control day). Wildfire \PM was modeled using a linear term per \SI[per-mode=symbol]{10}{\micro\gram\per\cubic\metre}.

\subsubsection{Dual exposure}
We examined PSPS events and wildfire \PM exposures by zip code-day to quantify the extent of dual exposure to PSPS events and wildfire \PM in our study population.

\subsubsection{Temperature data}
We used daily maximum temperature data from the Parameter-elevation Regressions on Independent Slopes Model (PRISM) at the zip code level.\citep{prism2014}

Additionally, we isolated chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and modeled it both as a component of respiratory outcomes and by itself, as a high proportion of COPD patients rely on electronic durable medical equipment -- a particular concern during a power outage.\citep{casey2020power, mcbrien_wildfire_2023}

The date of hospital admission or emergency department visit was considered the case-day. To generate a dataset of control-days, we selected control days for every case-day by matching on zip code, day of week, and month. In other words, if a case occurred on a Monday in January, all other Mondays in January \textit{except} for the day of the hospital encounter, were considered control days for that individual.

We linked exposure data to hospital admissions and emergency department visits using zip code and date.\citep{hcai_datasets}

\subsection{Statistical analysis}
This study examined the association between PSPS events with and without co-occurring wildfire \PM and respiratory, cardiovascular, and psychiatric hospitalizations and emergency department encounters among adults . By allowing every case to serve as their own control in a time-stratified case-crossover design, we estimated the effect of a PSPS event alone as well as a PSPS event occurring at the same time as wildfire \PM on these four causes. This approach is commonly used in epidemiologic studies of air pollution as it inherently controls for time-invariant individual-level factors, such as demographics and comorbidities, and temporal variation, such as day of week effects and seasonality.\citep{bateson2004sensitive, carracedo2010case, janes2005case, schwarz2024effects} The primary analysis examines the effects of PSPS event defined as mild, moderate, or severe based on the total count of customers impacted by the event. A secondary analysis examined the effects of PSPS event defined as mild, moderate, or severe based on the relative number of customers impacted by the event (explained further in supplement). 

With our time-stratified case-crossover design, we examined the association between a (1) PSPS event alone, (2) wildfire \PM alone, and (3) dual exposure to a PSPS event and wildfire \PM and hospital admissions and emergency department visits. 

We estimated effects using a conditional logistic regression model for each health endpoint. Each model included PSPS event exposure, cumulative lagged wildfire \PM exposure, an interaction term between PSPS event and wildfire smoke exposure, and a spline on daily temperature. Additionally, we used controlled for daily maximum temperature, modeled with a natural spline with 4 knots.\citep{maclure1991case, schwarz2024effects}
