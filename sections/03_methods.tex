\section{Methods}
\subsection{Study population}
In this zip code-level time-stratified case-crossover\citep{lumley2000bias} study in California, we used data on hospital admissions and emergency department visits among adults $\geq$ 20 years of age from 2013 -- 2019 from the California Department of Health Care Access and Information (HCAI), \citep{hcai_datasets} across 1,721 California zip codes where we had access to power outage data and at least one healthcare encounter occurred (97\% of zip codes) (figure \ref{fig1}). Based on prior power outage and wildfire literature, we studied all respiratory, COPD, cardiovascular, and psychiatric healthcare encounters.\citep{casey2020power, casey2024measuring} This study was approved by the California Health and Human Services Agency’s Committee for the Protection of Human Subjects (project number 2021-116).

\subsection{Exposures and covariates}

\subsubsection{PSPS data}
We measured exposure to PSPS events from 2013--2022 using data from PSE Healthy Energy, which compiled and mapped historic PSPS events from California's three major energy providers, Pacific Gas and Electric (PG\&E), San Diego Gas and Electric (SDG\&E), and Southern California Edison (SCE).\citep{cpuc_psps_reports, pse_healthy_energy, cpuc_psps_2025} 

The PSPS dataset contains circuit-level information for each PSPS event across the three major utilities (PG\&E, SDG\&E or SCE). A circuit is defined as XXXX. A PSPS event may impact multiple circuits, so each circuit-event had a start time, end time, duration, and number of residential and commercial customers impacted.\citep{pse_healthy_energy, cpuc_psps_2025} Utilities may not de-energize an entire circuit and circuits may be energized and de-energized multiple times throughout events. Because we lacked information on these two characteristics of outages, we assumed that the number of people affected were evenly spread across the circuit and that the entire circuit was without power for the duration of the PSPS event. 

We overlaid these circuit-level data containing the number of customers affected\citep{pse_healthy_energy, cpuc_psps_2025} with high-resolution gridded estimates of population from the 2020 census in California\citep{depsky2022high} to estimate the (1) the number of people theoretically served by the circuit, and (2) the number of people affected and the number of people served at the zip code-level. We used this information to derive our exposure of interest, the total number of customers impacted each day of the PSPS event for each zip code. Based on tertiles of these counts, we categorized PSPS events as mild, moderate, or severe for each zip code-event. Most events exceeded a day, and the median duration was \medianduration{} hours. The PSPS tertiles of exposure were therefore calculated at the zip code-event level to ensure equal weighting of longer and shorter events and retain the basis of the severity evaluation being on number of people rather than duration.

Because of highly variable population counts within zip codes, we also constructed a secondary metric meant to measure the relative severity of the PSPS event. This metric was computed by multiplying the total number of customers without power by the proportion of customers affected in each zip code. We again created tertiles of exposure at the zip code-event level.

\subsubsection{Wildfire smoke data}
We used daily wildfire \PM estimates at the zip code level.\citep{aguilera2023novel} The estimates were modeled for ZIP Code Tabulation Areas (ZCTAs) by using a set of machine learning models and multiple predictor variables, including air quality monitor data, aerosol optical depth, land cover, and meteorological conditions, to produce daily PM2.5 concentrations.\citep{aguilera2023novel} Wildfire-specific PM2.5 was then calculated by imputing non-wildfire-related PM2.5 concentrations using a chained random forest algorithm and taking the difference between these values and the modeled PM2.5 concentrations. We averaged wildfire \PM exposure across the 4 days prior to and the day of interest (either a case or control day)).

\subsubsection{Co-occurring exposure}
We examined PSPS events and wildfire \PM exposures by zip code-day to quantify the extent of co-occurring PSPS events and wildfire \PM in our study population. Hereafter, we refer to this co-occurrence as co-exposure.

\subsubsection{Temperature data}
We leveraged gridded (4km grid) daily temperature data extracted at the location of population-weighted centroids for zip codes from gridMET.\citep{abatzoglou2013development} 

\subsection{Outcomes}
Our outcome data relied on HCAI individual-level hospital admission and emergency department visit data from 2013--2019, which included date of admission, associated diagnoses based on \textit{International Classification of Disease, Ninth Edition} (\textit{ICD}-9) and Tenth Edition (\textit{ICD}-10) codes, hospital zip code, and patient’s residential zip code. When a patient's zip code was missing, we used the zip code of the hospital (n=XX cases). 

Using the primary diagnosis codes, we identified daily all respiratory, COPD, cardiovascular, and psychiatric hospital admission and emergency department encounters. Diagnostic codes outside the primary position were not used. We chose these cause groups based on previous research of power outages and health.\citep{casey2020power} \textit{ICD} codes used in this analysis are in table \ref{tab:icd_codes}. We included unscheduled hospitalizations (unplanned/emergency) and excluded scheduled visits (e.g., planned/elective admissions). We excluded cases among those 19 years and younger. 

Additionally, we isolated chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and modeled it both as a component of respiratory outcomes and by itself, as a high proportion of COPD patients rely on electronic durable medical equipment -- a particular concern during a power outage.\citep{casey2020power, mcbrien_wildfire_2023}

\subsection{Statistical analysis}
This study examined the association between PSPS events with and without co-occurring wildfire \PM and all respiratory, COPD, cardiovascular, and psychiatric hospitalizations and emergency department encounters among adults. Patient index and referent days in this case-crossover study were linked to exposure data using zip code and date. The date of hospital admission or emergency department visit was considered the index day. To generate a dataset of referent days, we selected referent days for every index day by matching on zip code, day of week, month, and year. In other words, if a index encounter occurred on a Monday in January of 2015, all other Mondays in January of 2015 \textit{except} for the day of the hospital encounter, were considered referent days for that individual.

We estimated the effect of a PSPS event alone as well as a PSPS event co-occurring with wildfire \PM on these four causes. While PSPS events usually last more than 24 hours, we considered only same-day exposure with healthcare encounters to identify case-days. Case-crossover studies are commonly used to evaluate short-term exposures and acute outcomes as they inherently control for time-invariant confounding factors; the time-stratified referent days control for day of week and seasonality.\citep{bateson2004sensitive, carracedo2010case, janes2005case, schwarz2024effects} The primary analysis examined the effects of severe PSPS events using both absolute and relative metrics. A secondary analysis examined mild and moderate PSPS events.

We examined the association between a (1) PSPS event alone, (2) wildfire \PM alone, and (3) co-exposure to a PSPS event and wildfire \PM and cause-specific hospital admissions and emergency department visits using a conditional logistic regression model for each health endpoint, accounting for the matched strata of case and controls. Each model included PSPS event exposure, 5-day average wildfire \PM exposure modeled using a linear term per \SI[per-mode=symbol]{10}{\micro\gram\per\cubic\metre}, an interaction term between PSPS event and wildfire \PM exposure to assess multiplicative interaction, and a natural cubic spline with three degrees of freedom on daily maximum temperature (selected based on best model fit to account for the potential non-linear relationship between temperature and health outcomes).\citep{maclure1991case, schwarz2024effects} We assessed interaction in two ways: (1) multiplicative scale interaction effect - the deviation from the expected combined effects of PSPS and wildfire \PM on a multiplicative scale; vs. (2) combined effect - total risk of both hazards when they occur (cite sup methods with equation).

R version 4.4.2 and Python version 3.13.0 were used for all analyses. Code is available at https://github.com/laurenwilner/psps\_ca\_analysis.
