\section{Introduction}
Climate change has accelerated at an unprecedented rate,\citep{nasa_evidence_2024} driving temperatures higher and increasing variability in precipitation patterns.\citep{westerling2006warming} Higher temperatures and prolonged droughts increase the risk of wildfires. Simultaneously, an increasing number of people living in high wildfire risk areas has elevated the number of people at risk.\citep{burke2021changing, marlon2012long, radeloff2018rapid} Coupled with increased fire suppression efforts yielding more available wildfire fuel,\citep{burke2021changing, marlon2012long} these factors have increased wildfire frequency, severity, and exposure in the Western United States.\citep{casey2024measuring, hurteau2014projected, dennison2014large, radeloff2018rapid, abatzoglou2016impact, burke2021changing} 

Wildfires impact human health and communities.\citep{casey2024measuring, moritz2014learning} Wildfires and the fine particulate (\PM) from wildfire smoke have been associated with adverse cardiorespiratory, neurological, and mental health outcomes.\citep{mcbrien_wildfire_2023, elser2025wildfire, epa_pm_2019, gould2024health, aguilera2021wildfire} Community-level effects of wildfires include displacement, damage and destruction of infrastructure, and power outages.\citep{gill2013worldwide, to2021impact, hamideh2022wildfire, grant2022long, gordon2025wildfire}

Increasingly, many wildfires not only affect the power grid, but many of the most devastating fires have been \textit{caused by} it. Power outages may cause wildfires when electrical infrastructure (e.g., power lines) comes into contact with trees and branches -- typically as a result of either the power line or the tree falling.\citep{sayarshad2023preignition} High winds cause falling tree branches on lines or that cause electric poles or lines to fall onto dry ground and ignite a fire.\citep{khan2022probabilistic, PSE2025} As such, in the event of winds strong enough to bring down power lines, should those lines ignite a fire, those same winds fuel that fire. For this reason, wildfires caused by electrical lines are among the most destructive.\citep{harding2024association, harding2025a}

Power outages alone have been associated with increased risk of adverse health outcomes, including injuries, hypothermia, hyperthermia, adverse cardiorespiratory outcomes, and mortality.\citep{casey2020power, northrop2024power, lin2021immediate, mcbrien2025} Together, wildfires and outages may increase adverse health outcomes by exposing individuals to more extreme temperatures in the absence of air conditioning and heating systems, by causing stress, by preventing individuals from using air filtration systems during events that compromise air quality, such as wildfires. To our knowledge, there have been no epidemiologic studies of this dual exposure.

As wildfire frequency increases, their co-occurrence with power outages is increasing.\citep{do2025spatiotemporal} In California, specifically, from 2018 -- 2020, the proportion of wildfires occurring at the same time as power outages ranged from 24\% -- 28\%, with an upward trend in this time period.\citep{do2025spatiotemporal} Given the negative health impacts of both these events, coupled with the unknown combined effects, this trend necessitates adopting adaptive measures to protect public safety. One such measure, implemented in California and increasingly adopted by other states, is Public Safety Power Shutoff (PSPS) events.\citep{psps_2025} During a PSPS event, electric utility companies proactively de-energize power lines to prevent them from causing or worsening wildfires.\citep{psps_2025} 

Intended to protect people, PSPS events may result in collateral adverse health effects.\citep{psps_2025} PSPS events carry the many of the same health risks as power outages, as they are simply planned outages. PSPS events may pose a particular threat when wildfires still occur despite the shutoff, exposing individuals to a dual hazard. This may happen if a PSPS event is (1) used to prevent a wildfire from worsening, or (2) initiated to attempt to reduce already high wildfire risk, but the wildfire still occurs. The dual hazard of a power outage and high concentrations of \PM may have greater than an additive adverse impact on health. Very limited studies have evaluated the behavioral and health impacts of PSPS events \PM.\citep{harding2025association, harding2024association, WONGPARODI2022102495, wong2022power, PSE2025} Previous research has indicated that similar to unplanned power outages, PSPS events disproportionately affect those who rely on electricity-dependent durable medical and assistive equipment (DME).\citep{PSE2025, pse_med_baseline} By compromising cell towers, PSPS events also risk health loss due to limited capacity for emergency communication.\citep{PSE2025} Finally, smaller medical facilities may lack generators and thus capacity for patient care may be reduced or eliminated.\citep{PSE2025} 


Despite the known health risks of wildfires and power outages independently, few studies have evaluated co-exposure to PSPS events and wildfire \PM.\citep{harding2025association, harding2024association, PSE2025} PSPS events are similar to power outages in many ways, but likely carry their own unique risk patterns. This study aimed to describe the extent of dual wildfire-PSPS exposure in California from 2013 -- 2019 and the association between the individual and dual exposures and adverse health outcomes.
