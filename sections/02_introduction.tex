\section{Introduction}
Climate change has accelerated at an unprecedented rate,\citep{nasa_evidence_2024} driving temperatures higher and increasing variability in precipitation patterns.\citep{westerling2006warming} These higher temperatures and prolonged droughts increase the risk of wildfires. Simultaneously, the number of people living in areas with high wildfire risk has grown.\citep{burke2021changing, marlon2012long, radeloff2018rapid} Coupled with increased fire suppression efforts yielding more available wildfire fuel,\citep{burke2021changing, marlon2012long} these factors have led to increased wildfire frequency, severity, and population exposure in the Western United States.\citep{casey2024measuring, hurteau2014projected, dennison2014large, radeloff2018rapid, abatzoglou2016impact, burke2021changing} 

Wildfires impact human health and communities.\citep{casey2024measuring, moritz2014learning} Residential proximity to wildfires and the fine particulate (\PM) from wildfire smoke have been associated with adverse cardiorespiratory, neurological, and mental health outcomes.\citep{mcbrien_wildfire_2023, elser2025wildfire, cleland2022short, epa_pm_2019, gould2024health, aguilera2021wildfire} Community-level effects of wildfires include population displacement, damage and destruction of homes and infrastructure, and power outages.\citep{gill2013worldwide, to2021impact, hamideh2022wildfire, grant2022long, gordon2025wildfire}

Increasingly, many wildfires not only impact the power grid but several of the most devastating have been \textit{caused by} it. Power outages may cause wildfires when electrical infrastructure (e.g., power lines) comes into contact with trees and branches--typically as a result of either the power line or the tree falling.\citep{sayarshad2023preignition} High winds knock down tree branches onto lines or electric poles or lines onto dry ground, where they can ignite a fire.\citep{khan2022probabilistic, PSE2025} When winds strong enough to bring down power lines blow, potentially igniting a fire,  those same winds can also fuel the flames and spread the fire. For this reason, wildfires caused by electrical lines can be among the most destructive.\citep{harding2024association, harding2025a}

Alone, power outages--likely due to stress, lack of indoor thermal control, reduced emergency and other communication, interrupted medical care, and cease of power to electricity-dependent durable medical equipment (DME)--have been associated with increased risk of adverse health outcomes, including injuries, hypothermia, hyperthermia, adverse cardiorespiratory outcomes, and mortality.\citep{casey2020power, northrop2024power, lin2021immediate, mcbrien2025, PSE2025} Together, wildfires and outages may increase risk of adverse health outcomes by causing more stress and preventing individuals from using air filtration systems to handle compromised air quality from wildfires. To our knowledge, there have been no epidemiological studies of this dual exposure. And while power outages alone may disproportionally impact DME users, when wildfire smoke is also present, those living with respiratory conditions such as COPD may be at especially elevated risk during poor air quality events when they cannot use air filtration or respiratory devices. 

As wildfires increase in frequency, their co-occurrence with power outages in California have increased.\citep{do2025spatiotemporal}  Given the negative health impacts of these events individually, coupled with their unknown combined effects, states have adopted adaptive measures to protect public safety. One such measure, implemented in California and increasingly adopted by other states, is Public Safety Power Shutoff (PSPS) events.\citep{psps_2025} During a PSPS event, electric utilities proactively de-energize power lines to prevent them from causing or worsening wildfires.\citep{psps_2025} 

Intended to protect people, PSPS events may result in collateral population health impacts.\citep{psps_2025} Studies have identified increased risk of motor vehicle crashes and reported stress following PSPS events.\citep{harding2025a, WONGPARODI2022102495} No studies have evaluated the health impacts of PSPS events, however health risks are likely similar to those from other power outages. PSPS events may pose an amplified threat when wildfires still occur despite the shutoff, dually exposing individuals. This may happen if a PSPS event is (1) used to prevent a wildfire from worsening, or (2) initiated to attempt to reduce already high wildfire risk, but a wildfire still occurs nearby. The dual hazard of a power outage and high concentrations of \PM may have synergistic health impacts that exceed the sum of their individuals effects. 

 This study aimed to describe the extent of dual PSPS-wildfire exposure in California from 2013--2019 and the association between the individual and dual exposures and adverse cardiovascular, respiratory, and psychiatric acute healthcare utilization.
