\section{Results}

\subsection{Health data description}

This study included 7,656,684, 591,315, 3,303,133, and 2,248,161 emergency department visits and hospital admissions among individuals $\geq$ 20 years old for a respiratory, COPD, cardiovascular, or psychiatric endpoint between 2013 -- 2019 in California (table \ref{traditional_table1}). \edperc{}\% of overall visits (\overalltotal{}) were emergency department encounters, while \haperc{}\% were unplanned hospital admissions (table \ref{ha_ed_table}).

\subsection{Respiratory morbidity}
We found that days with severe magnitude PSPS events and no wildfire smoke were associated with an elevated same-day odds of a respiratory emergency department visit or hospital admission (absolute: OR = \respabsorSameDay{}, 95\% CI: \respabscilowSameDay{}, \respabscihighSameDay{}) (figure \ref{results_fig}). Respiratory emergency department visits or hospital admissions were not elevated following lagged 0-3 day exposure. 

A \SI[per-mode=symbol]{10}{\micro\gram\per\cubic\metre} increase in same-day and \text{lag}$_{0\text{-}3}$ average wildfire \PM, in the absence of PSPS events, were each associated with higher odds of a respiratory emergency department visit or hospital admission (figure \ref{results_fig}, ADD OR + 95 CI for both).

Considering the joint effects, compared to no PSPS event and no wildfire \PM exposure, we estimated that same-day severe PSPS event co-occurring with \SI[per-mode=symbol]{10}{\micro\gram\per\cubic\metre} higher wildfire \PM was associated with higher odds of a respiratory emergency department visit or hospital admission (\respcombabspercSameDay\% [95\% CI: \respcombabslowSameDay{}, \respcombabshighSameDay{}]) (figure \ref{results_fig}). We found evidence of multiplicative interaction between severe PSPS events and wildfire \PM on respiratory emergency department visits or hospital admissions (\respintabsorSameDay [95\% CI: \respintabscilowSameDay{}, \respintabscihighSameDay{}]) (figure \ref{results_fig}). Results were similar for \text{lag}$_{0\text{-}3}$ exposures, where we detected evidence of multiplicative interaction between severe PSPS events and wildfire \PM on respiratory emergency department visits or hospital admissions (\respintabsorLagFour [95\% CI: \respintabscilowLagFour{}, \respintabscihighLagFour{}]) (figure \ref{results_fig}).

When we isolated COPD encounters, we observed stronger PSPS associations. We estimated that those exposed to a same-day severe magnitude PSPS event had \copdabsorSameDay{} (95\% CI: \copdabscilowSameDay{}, \copdabscihighSameDay{}) times higher odds of a COPD-related emergency department visit or hospital admission (figure \ref{results_fig}). Lagged effects were similar, but smaller in magnitude (\copdabsorLagFour{} [95\% CI: \copdabscilowLagFour{}, \copdabscihighLagFour{}]).

For COPD encounters alone, compared to those exposed to neither a severe magnitude PSPS event nor wildfire \PM, exposure to co-occurring PSPS event and \SI[per-mode=symbol]{10}{\micro\gram\per\cubic\metre} higher wildfire \PM was associated with higher odds of emergency department visits or hospital admissions (same-day OR = \copdcombabsorSameDay{}, 95\% CI: \copdcombabslowSameDay{}, \copdcombabshighSameDay{}; \text{lag}$_{0\text{-}3}$ day OR = \copdcombabsor{}, 95\% CI: \copdcombabslow{}, \copdcombabshigh{} figure \ref{results_fig}). There was potential evidence of multiplicative interaction between severe PSPS events and wildfire \PM exposure that was more apparent for \text{lag}$_{0\text{-}3}$ exposures (\copdintabsorLagFour \phantom{ } [95\% CI: \copdintabscilowLagFour{}, \copdintabscihighLagFour{}]). 

Respiratory effects were consistent across all age strata, however, evidence of multiplicative interaction was present for those ages 20-64 years, but not in older adults (figures \ref{results_fig_20_64_abs}, \ref{results_fig_20_64_hyb}, \ref{results_fig_65_plus_abs}, and \ref{results_fig_65_plus_hyb}). When isolating COPD, the effect of severe PSPS event, as well as the joint effect of severe PSPS event and wildfire \PM, were stronger in adults ages 65 years and older (figures \ref{results_fig_20_64_abs}, \ref{results_fig_20_64_hyb}, \ref{results_fig_65_plus_abs}, and \ref{results_fig_65_plus_hyb}).

\subsection{Cardiovascular morbidity and psychiatric disorders}
We did not observe an association between same-day or \text{lag}$_{0\text{-}3}$ mild, moderate, or severe magnitude PSPS events alone, wildfire \PM exposure alone, or co-occurring PSPS events and wildfire \PM exposure and cardiovascular-related or psychiatric disorder-related emergency department visits or hospitalizations (figures \ref{results_fig} and \ref{zctas_included_map}). This was consistent in age-stratified models as well ((figures \ref{results_fig_20_64_abs}, \ref{results_fig_20_64_hyb}, \ref{results_fig_65_plus_abs}, and \ref{results_fig_65_plus_hyb}).

\subsection{Secondary analysis}
In a secondary analysis, we assessed a relative metric of exposure to PSPS events (tables \ref{supp_exposure_table_same_day} and \ref{supp_exposure_table_lag4}), and results were similar to those of the absolute exposure metric (figures \ref{results_fig_supplement_abs} and \ref{results_fig_supplement_hyb}). We also examined mild and moderate magnitude PSPS events, and neither they nor their interaction with wildfire \PM exposure were associated with any type of emergency department visits or hospital admission (figure \ref{zctas_included_map}).

\subsection{Sensitivity analysis}
In a sensitivity analysis using a lag 0-6 day PSPS duration metric, we did not observe any association between PSPS duration and any healthcare encounters. We found modest evidence of multiplicative interaction between 8-hour longer PSPS duration and wildfire \PM for respiratory- and COPD-related encounters for those under 65 years of age (respiratory OR = \respintorOneWk{}, 95\% CI: [\respintcilowOneWk{}, \respintcihighOneWk{}]; COPD OR = \copdintorOneWk{}, 95\% CI: [\copdintcilowOneWk{}, \copdintcihighOneWk{}]) (figures \ref{1week_duration_fig}, \ref{1week_duration_fig_young}). Results were null for older adults (figure \ref{1week_duration_fig_old}).
