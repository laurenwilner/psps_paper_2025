Across these providers, we included \npspsevents{} PSPS events. The median duration of a PSPS event was \medianduration{} hours. During the 7-year study period, there were \zipdays{} zip code-days with PSPS events, and each zip code experienced, on average, \zipevents{} events (figure \ref{fig1}), and the most common classification was '\mostcommonsevabs{}'. Overall, \zipcodeswithevents{} of the \cazipcodes{} zip codes in California experienced at least one PSPS event in the study period (figure \ref{supp_fig1}).

\subsection{Respiratory morbidity}
Using both our relative and absolute metrics, we found that severe PSPS event exposure was associated with an elevated same-day odds of a respiratory hospital or emergency department encounter (relative: OR = \resphybor{}, 95\% confidence interval [CI]: \resphybcilow{}, \resphybcihigh{}; absolute: OR = \respabsor{}, 95\% CI: \respabscilow{}, \respabscihigh{}) (figure \ref{fig2}).

We found that exposure to \SI[per-mode=symbol]{10}{\micro\gram\per\cubic\metre} higher wildfire \PM  over a 5-day lag period was associated with a \wfsabsperc\% (95\% CI: \wfsabscilow, \wfsabscihigh) higher odds of a respiratory hospital or emergency department encounter (figure \ref{fig2}).


Considering co-exposure, we found evidence of a multiplicative impact of co-occurring severe PSPS events and 10 \SI[per-mode=symbol]{10}{\micro\gram\per\cubic\metre} higher wildfire \PM on respiratory hospital or emergency department encounters (relative: \respinthybor [95\% CI: \respinthybcilow{}, \respinthybcihigh{}]; absolute: \respintabsor [95\% CI: \respintabscilow{}, \respintabscihigh{}]) (figure \ref{fig2}). Compared to no PSPS and no wildfire \PM exposure, we estimated that the odds of a respiratory hospital or emergency department encounter were \respcombhybperc\% (95\% CI: \respcombhyblow{}, \respcombhybhigh{}) and \respcombabsperc\% (95\% CI: \respcombabslow{}, \respcombabshigh{}) higher among those exposed to co-occurring PSPS event and 10 \SI[per-mode=symbol]{10}{\micro\gram\per\cubic\metre} of wildfire \PM according to our relative and absolute metrics respectively (figure \ref{fig2}). 

When we isolated COPD, we saw stronger effects. We estimated that those exposed to a severe PSPS event had \copdhybor{} (95\% CI: \copdhybcilow{}, \copdhybcihigh{}) higher odds of COPD-related hospital or emergency department encounter according to our relative metric, and \copdabsor{} (95\% CI: \copdabscilow{}, \copdabscihigh{}) times higher odds according to our absolute metric (figure \ref{fig2}).

For COPD alone, there was also potential evidence of multiplicative interaction between severe PSPS event and 10 \SI[per-mode=symbol]{10}{\micro\gram\per\cubic\metre} higher wildfire \PM, but confidence intervals were wide (relative: \copdinthybor [95\% CI: \copdinthybcilow{}, \copdinthybcihigh{}]; absolute: \copdintabsor [95\% CI: \copdintabscilow{}, \copdintabscihigh{}]). Further, compared to those exposed to neither a severe PSPS event nor wildfire \PM, the odds of COPD-related hospital or emergency department encounter were \copdcombhybor{} (95\% CI: \copdcombhyblow{}, \copdcombhybhigh{}) times higher according to our relative metric, and \copdcombabsor{} (95\% CI: \copdcombabslow{}, \copdcombabshigh{}) times higher according to our absolute metric (figure \ref{fig2}). 

Neither mild nor moderate PSPS event exposure was associated with overall respiratory hospital or emergency department encounters; point estimates were elevated for COPD-specific encounters, but confidence intervals were wide (figure \ref{supp_fig1}).

\subsection{Cardiovascular morbidity and psychiatric disorders}
We did not observe an association between mild, moderate, or severe PSPS events or co-occurring PSPS events and wildfire \PM and cardiovascular-related or psychiatric disorder-related hospitalizations or emergency department encounters (figures \ref{fig2} and \ref{supp_fig1}).
