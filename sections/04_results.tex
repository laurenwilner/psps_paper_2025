\section{Results}

\subsection{Health data description}

This study examined the effects of PSPS events with and without co-occurring wildfire \PM on respiratory, cardiovascular, and psychiatric hospitalizations and emergency department encounters among adults . By allowing every case to serve as their own control in a time-stratified case-crossover design, we estimated the effect of a PSPS event alone as well as a PSPS event occurring at the same time as wildfire \PM on these four causes. The primary analysis examines the effects of PSPS event defined as mild, moderate, or severe based on the total count of customers impacted by the event. A secondary analysis examined the effects of PSPS event defined as mild, moderate, or severe based on the relative number of customers impacted by the event (explained further in supplement).

This study included XX adults $\geq$ 20 years old who had a hospital admission or emergency department visit between 2013 -- 2019 in California. Of the health endpoints included in this study, the most common was respiratory (n = 4,923,047 case-days). Using these case-days, we generated 16,676,351 control-days. We identified the greatest number of encounters associated with the severe exposure level, with 1,717 cardiovascular case-days during a severe outage and 817 respiratory case-days. We accordingly identified 6,159 and 2,892 control-days for cardiovascular and respiratory encounters, respectively (\ref{table1}).

\subsection{Psychiatric disorders}
All findings for the association between PSPS events and psychiatric disorders were null. Additionally, results for the association between PSPS events that co-occur with wildfire \PM were also null. Though previous studies have found a relationship between wildfire \PM and psychiatric disorders such as dementia,\citep{elser2025wildfire} we did not have a large enough sample size to detect this relationship in isolation.
