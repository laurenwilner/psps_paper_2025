PSPS events cover customers served by the three major energy providers in California -- PG\&E, San Diego Gas and Electric SDG\&E, and Southern California Edison SCE,\citep{cpuc_psps_reports} which serve approximately XX\% of California. Across these providers, we included \npspsevents{} PSPS events in this study. The median duration of a PSPS event was \medianduration{} hours. There were \zipdays{} zip code -days in our 7-year study period that experienced PSPS events, and each zip code experienced, on average, \zipevents{} events (figure \ref{fig2}). \mostcommonsevabs{} (followed by \secondmostcommonsevabs{}) was the most common when we used our absolute metric, and the same was true according to our relative metric. Overall, \zipcodeswithevents{} of the \cazipcodes{} zip codes in California experienced at least one PSPS event in the study period and were included in this study (figure \ref{fig1}).

We examined our secondary exposure, wildfire smoke \PM, independent and concurrent with PSPS events. There were \zipcodedayswf{} zip code-days of wildfire smoke \PM from 2013 -- 2019 and \zipdaysdualexp{} zip code-days of co-occurring wildfire smoke \PM and PSPS events during the same period (figure \ref{fig2}). The mean wildfire smoke \PM across the study period was \meanwfpm{} \SI[per-mode=symbol]{10}{\micro\gram\per\cubic\metre}. 

Using the HCAI data and our absolute PSPS metric, we identified \casedaysabs{} case-days and \controldaysabs{} control-days across mild, moderate, and severe exposure for our absolute metric, and \casedayshyb{} case-days and \controldayshyb{} control-days across mild, moderate, and severe exposure for our relative metric (table \ref{table2}). We identified the greatest number of encounters associated with the severe exposure level, with \casedaysabssevcvd{} cardiovascular case-days during a severe outage and \casedaysabssevresp{} respiratory case-days (table \ref{table2}). Using our relative metric, we identified the greatest number of encounters associated with the \highestencountersexplevel{} exposure level, with \respcasedayssev respiratory case-days during a severe outage, \cvdcasedayssev{} cardiovascular case-days, and \psychcasedayssev{} psychiatric case days (table \ref{table2}).

\subsection{Respiratory morbidity}
While odds of a respiratory hospital or emergency department encounter was not significantly higher (absolute metric OR: \respabsor{}, 95\% CI: \respabscilow{}, \respabscihigh{}; hybrid metric OR: \resphybor{}, 95\% CI: \resphybcilow{}, \resphybcihigh{}) in those exposed to severe PSPS events than those who were not exposed, the odds of a respiratory visit were \respintabsor{} (95\% CI: \respintabscilow{}, \respintabscihigh{}) times higher among individuals exposed to a co-occurring severe PSPS event and wildfire \PM according to our absolute metric. Similarly, according to our relative metric, odds were \respinthybor{} times higher (95\% CI: \respinthybcilow{}, \respinthybcihigh{}) (figure \ref{fig3}). When we isolated COPD, we saw the inverse. The odds of a COPD-related hospital or emergency department encounter were \copdabsor{} (95\% CI: \copdabscilow{}, \copdabscihigh{}) times higher among those exposed to a severe PSPS event according to our absolute metric, and \copdhybor{} (95\% CI: \copdhybcilow{}, \copdhybcihigh{}) times higher among those exposed to a severe PSPS event according to our relative metric. However, we found null effects among those exposed to co-occurring severe PSPS events and wildfire \PM (figure \ref{fig3}). 

\subsection{Cardiovascular morbidity and Psychiatric disorders}
All findings for the association between PSPS events and psychiatric disorders were null. Additionally, results for the association between PSPS events that co-occur with wildfire \PM were also null. Though previous studies have found a relationship between wildfire \PM and psychiatric disorders such as dementia,\citep{elser2025wildfire} we did not have a large enough sample size to detect this relationship in isolation (\ref{fig1}).
