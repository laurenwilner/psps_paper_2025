\subsection{Health data description}

This study included 7,656,684, 591,315, 3,303,133, and 2,248,161 emergency department visits and hospital admissions among individuals $\geq$ 20 years old for a respiratory, COPD, cardiovascular, or psychiatric endpoint between 2013 -- 2019 in California (table \ref{traditional_table1}). \edperc{}\% of overall visits (\overalltotal{}) were emergency department encounters, while \haperc{}\% were hospital admissions (table \ref{ha_ed_table}).

\subsection{Respiratory morbidity}
We found that days with severe magnitude PSPS events and no wildfire smoke were associated with an elevated same-day odds of a respiratory emergency department visit or hospital admission (absolute: OR = \respabsor{}, 95\% CI: \respabscilow{}, \respabscihigh{}) (figure \ref{results_fig}).

\begin{wraptable}{r}{0.45\textwidth}
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.45\textwidth]{tables_figures/exposure_table.png}
  \caption{Cases by exposure status. PSPS event categories (mild/moderate/severe) are tertiles for each metric. PSPS events were defined as mild for those events affecting $\leq$ 47 people, moderate for those where the number of customers without power was $>$ 47 and $\leq$ 468, and severe for those where $>$ 468 customers were without power.}
  \vspace{-2em}
  \label{exposure_table}
\end{wraptable}

A 10 ug/m3 increase in 5-day average wildfire \PM, when no PSPS events occurred on the index day, was associated with a \wfsabsperc\% (95\% CI: \wfsabscilow, \wfsabscihigh) higher odds of a respiratory emergency department visit or hospital admission (figure \ref{results_fig}).



Considering the joint effects, compared to no PSPS event and no wildfire \PM exposure, we estimated that a severe PSPS event co-occurring with \SI[per-mode=symbol]{10}{\micro\gram\per\cubic\metre} higher wildfire \PM was associated with higher odds of a respiratory emergency department visit or hospital admission (\respcombabsperc\% [95\% CI: \respcombabslow{}, \respcombabshigh{}]) (figure \ref{results_fig}). We found evidence of multiplicative interaction between severe PSPS events and wildfire \PM on respiratory emergency department visits or hospital admissions (\respintabsor [95\% CI: \respintabscilow{}, \respintabscihigh{}]) (figure \ref{results_fig}). 

When we isolated COPD encounters, we saw stronger associations. We estimated that those exposed to a severe magnitude PSPS event had \copdabsor{} (95\% CI: \copdabscilow{}, \copdabscihigh{}) times higher odds of a COPD-related emergency department visit or hospital admission (figure \ref{results_fig}).

For COPD encounters alone, compared to those exposed to neither a severe magnitude PSPS event nor wildfire \PM, exposure to co-occurring PSPS event and \SI[per-mode=symbol]{10}{\micro\gram\per\cubic\metre} higher wildfire \PM was associated with higher odds of emergency department visits or hospital admissions (OR = \copdcombabsor{}, 95\% CI: \copdcombabslow{}, \copdcombabshigh{}, figure \ref{results_fig}). There was potential evidence of multiplicative interaction between severe PSPS events and wildfire \PM exposure (\copdintabsor \phantom{ } [95\% CI: \copdintabscilow{}, \copdintabscihigh{}]). 

\subsection{Cardiovascular morbidity and psychiatric disorders}
We did not observe an association between mild, moderate, or severe magnitude PSPS events alone, wildfire \PM exposure alone, or co-occurring PSPS events and wildfire \PM exposure and cardiovascular-related or psychiatric disorder-related emergency department visits or hospitalizations (figures \ref{results_fig} and \ref{zctas_included_map}).   

\subsection{Secondary analysis}
In a secondary analysis, we assessed a relative metric of exposure to PSPS events (table \ref{supp_exposure_table}) and results were similar to the absolute exposure metric (figure \ref{results_fig_supplement}). We also examined mild and moderate magnitude PSPS events, and neither them nor their interaction with wildfire \PM exposure, were associated with any type of emergency department visits or hospital admission (figure \ref{zctas_included_map}).
