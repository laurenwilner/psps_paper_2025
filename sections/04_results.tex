\subsection{Respiratory morbidity}
While odds of a respiratory hospital or emergency department encounter was not significantly higher (absolute metric OR: XXX, 95\CI: XXX, XXX; hybrid metric OR: XX, 95\% CI: XX, XX) in those exposed to severe PSPS events than those who were not exposed, the odds of a respiratory visit were XXX times higher among individuals exposed to a co-occurring severe PSPS event and wildfire \PM according to our absolute metric. Similarly, according to our relative metric, odds were XX times higher (95\% CI: XX, XX) (figure \ref{fig3}). When we isolated COPD, we saw the inverse. The odds of a COPD-related hospital or emergency department encounter were XX times higher among those exposed to a severe PSPS event according to our absolute metric, and XX times higher among those exposed to a severe PSPS event according to our relative metric. However, we found null effects among those exposed to co-occurring severe PSPS events and wildfire \PM (figure \ref{fig3}). 

\subsection{Cardiovascular mobidity and Psychiatric disorders}
All findings for the association between PSPS events and psychiatric disorders were null. Additionally, results for the association between PSPS events that co-occur with wildfire \PM were also null. Though previous studies have found a relationship between wildfire \PM and psychiatric disorders such as dementia,\citep{elser2025wildfire} we did not have a large enough sample size to detect this relationship in isolation (\ref{fig1}).
