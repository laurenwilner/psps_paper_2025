\subsection{Respiratory morbidity}
Using both our relative and absolute metrics, we found that those exposed to a severe PSPS event had elevated same-day odds of a respiratory hospital or emergency department encounter (relative: OR = \resphybor{}, 95\% confidence interval (CI): \resphybcilow{}, \resphybcihigh{}; absolute: OR = \respabsor{}, 95\% CI: \respabscilow{}, \respabscihigh{}) (figure \ref{fig3}).

We found that exposure to 10 \SI[per-mode=symbol]{10}{\micro\gram\per\cubic\metre} higher wildfire \PM over a 5-day lag period was associated with a [INSERT OR AND CI] 1\% higher odds of a respiratory hospital or emergency department encounter (figure \ref{fig3}).

Additionally, we found evidence of multiplicative interaction (relative: \respinthybor [95\% CI: \respinthybcilow{}, \respinthybcihigh{}]; absolute: \respintabsor [95\% CI: \respintabscilow{}, \respintabscihigh{}]) (figure \ref{fig3}). 

Compared to no PSPS and no wildfire \PM exposure, we estimated that the odds of a respiratory hospital or emergency department encounter were 22\% (95\% CI: \respcombhyblow{}, \respcombhybhigh{}) and 18\% (95\% CI: \respcombabslow{}, \respcombabshigh{}) higher among those exposed to co-occurring PSPS event and 10 \SI[per-mode=symbol]{10}{\micro\gram\per\cubic\metre} of wildfire \PM according to our relative and absolute metrics respectively (figure \ref{fig3}). 

When we isolated COPD, we saw stronger effects. We estimated that those exposed to a severe PSPS event had \copdhybor{} (95\% CI: \copdhybcilow{}, \copdhybcihigh{}) higher odds of COPD-related hospital or emergency department encounter according to our relative metric, and \copdabsor{} (95\% CI: \copdabscilow{}, \copdabscihigh{}) times higher odds according to our absolute metric (figure \ref{fig3}).

For COPD alone, there is also potential evidence of multiplicative interaction between severe PSPS event and wildfire \PM, but confidence intervals were wide [INSERT INTERACTION TERM OR AND CI for abs and rel]. Further, compared to those exposed to neither a severe PSPS event nor wildfire \PM, the odds of COPD-related hospital or emergency department encounter were \copdcombhybor{} (95\% CI: \copdcombhyblow{}, \copdcombhybhigh{}) times higher according to our relative metric, and \copdcombabsor{} (95\% CI: \copdcombabslow{}, \copdcombabshigh{}) times higher according to our absolute metric (figure \ref{fig3}). 

\subsection{Cardiovascular morbidity and psychiatric disorders}
All findings for the association between PSPS events and psychiatric disorders were null. Additionally, results for the association between PSPS events that co-occur with wildfire \PM were also null. Though previous studies have found a relationship between wildfire \PM and psychiatric disorders such as dementia,\citep{elser2025wildfire} we did not have a large enough sample size to detect this relationship in isolation (\ref{fig1}).
