\section{Results}

\subsection{Health data description}

This study included 7,656,684, 591,315, 3,303,133, and 2,248,161 hospital admissions or emergency department visits among individuals $\geq$ 20 years old for a respiratory, COPD, cardiovascular, or psychiatric endpoint between 2013 -- 2019 in California. Respiratory visits were most commonly observed (n = 7,656,684), followed by cardiovascular and psychiatric visits. X\% of overall visits were ED and Y\% were hospital admissions (Table INSERT TABLE ONCE I HAVE THEM). 

During the 7-year study period, there were \zipdays{} zip code-days with PSPS events (figure \ref{fig1}), and most were classified as '\mostcommonsevabs{}'. Overall, \zipcodeswithevents{} of the \cazipcodes{} zip codes in California experienced at least one PSPS event in the study period (figure \ref{supp_fig1}). Most PSPS events occurred in the summer and fall (supp figure cite).

\subsection{Respiratory morbidity}
Using both our relative and absolute metrics, we found that severe PSPS exposure on days with no wildfire smoke was associated with an elevated same-day odds of a respiratory hospital or emergency department encounter (relative: OR = \resphybor{}, 95\% confidence interval [CI]: \resphybcilow{}, \resphybcihigh{}; absolute: OR = \respabsor{}, 95\% CI: \respabscilow{}, \respabscihigh{}) (figure \ref{fig2}).

A 10 ug/m3 increase in 5-day average wildfire PM2.5, when no PSPS events occurred on the event day, was associated with a \wfsabsperc\% (95\% CI: \wfsabscilow, \wfsabscihigh) higher odds of a respiratory hospital or emergency department encounter (figure \ref{fig2}).

Considering co-exposure, compared to no PSPS event and no wildfire \PM exposure, we estimated that those exposed to a co-occurring PSPS event and \SI[per-mode=symbol]{10}{\micro\gram\per\cubic\metre} higher wildfire \PM had higher odds of a respiratory hospital or emergency department encounter (relative: \respcombhybperc\% [95\% CI: \respcombhyblow{}, \respcombhybhigh{}]; absolute: \respcombabsperc\% [95\% CI: \respcombabslow{}, \respcombabshigh{}]) (figure \ref{fig2}). We found evidence that the relationship between co-occurring severe PSPS events and wildfire \PM on respiratory hospital or emergency department encounters was multiplicative (relative: \respinthybor [95\% CI: \respinthybcilow{}, \respinthybcihigh{}]; absolute: \respintabsor [95\% CI: \respintabscilow{}, \respintabscihigh{}]) (figure \ref{fig2}). 

When we isolated COPD encounters, we saw stronger associations. We estimated that those exposed to a severe PSPS event had \copdhybor{} (95\% CI: \copdhybcilow{}, \copdhybcihigh{}) higher odds of a COPD-related hospital or emergency department encounter according to our relative metric, and \copdabsor{} (95\% CI: \copdabscilow{}, \copdabscihigh{}) times higher odds according to our absolute metric (figure \ref{fig2}).

For COPD encounters alone, compared to those exposed to neither a severe PSPS event nor wildfire \PM, we estimated that those exposed to a co-occurring PSPS event and \SI[per-mode=symbol]{10}{\micro\gram\per\cubic\metre} higher wildfire \PM had higher odds of COPD-related hospital or emergency department encounter were \copdcombhybor{} (95\% CI: \copdcombhyblow{}, \copdcombhybhigh{}) times higher according to our relative metric, and \copdcombabsor{} (95\% CI: \copdcombabslow{}, \copdcombabshigh{}) times higher according to our absolute metric (figure \ref{fig2}). There was also potential evidence of multiplicative interaction between severe PSPS events and wildfire \PM exposure, but confidence intervals were wide (relative: \copdinthybor [95\% CI: \copdinthybcilow{}, \copdinthybcihigh{}]; absolute: \copdintabsor [95\% CI: \copdintabscilow{}, \copdintabscihigh{}]). 

In a secondary analysis, we assessed a relative metric of exposure to PSPS events and results were similar to the absolute exposure metric [CITE SUPPLEMENTARY FIGS].

Neither mild nor moderate PSPS event exposure, nor their interaction with wildfire \PM exposure, was associated with overall respiratory or COPD-specific hospital or emergency department encounters (figure \ref{supp_fig1}).

\subsection{Cardiovascular morbidity and psychiatric disorders}
We did not observe an association between mild, moderate, or severe PSPS events alone, wildfire \PM exposure alone, or co-occurring PSPS events and wildfire \PM exposure and cardiovascular-related or psychiatric disorder-related hospitalizations or emergency department encounters (figures \ref{fig2} and \ref{supp_fig1}).
