\section{Results}

\subsection{Health data description}

This study included 7,656,684, 591,315, 3,303,133, and 2,248,161 hospital admissions or emergency department visits among individuals $\geq$ 20 years old for a respiratory, COPD, cardiovascular, or psychiatric endpoint between 2013 -- 2019 in California (table \ref{traditional_table1}). Respiratory visits were most commonly observed (n = 7,656,684), followed by cardiovascular and psychiatric visits. X\% of overall visits were ED and Y\% were hospital admissions (Table INSERT TABLE ONCE I HAVE THEM). 

During the 7-year study period, there were \zipdays{} zip code-days with PSPS events (figure \ref{map_violin}), and most were classified as '\mostcommonsevabs{}'. Overall, \zipcodeswithevents{} of the \cazipcodes{} zip codes in California experienced at least one PSPS event in the study period (figure \ref{zctas_included_map}). Most PSPS events occurred in the summer and fall \ref{seasonality_plot}.

We examined our secondary exposure, wildfire \PM, independent from and concurrent with PSPS events. The mean daily wildfire \PM concentration across the study period was \meanwfpm{} \SI[per-mode=symbol]{10}{\micro\gram\per\cubic\metre}. There were \zipcodedayswf{} (\percentzipcodedayswf\%) zip code-days where there was any wildfire \PM from 2013 -- 2019 and \zipdaysdualexp{} zip code-days with any wildfire \PM and a PSPS event (figure \ref{map_violin}). 

We identified \pspsexpresp{} respiratory, \pspsexpcopd{} COPD, \pspsexpcardio{} cardiovascular, \pspsexppsych{} psychiatric index days exposed to a PSPS event (mild, moderate, or severe) (table \ref{exposure_table}).

\subsection{Respiratory morbidity}
We found that days with severe magnitude PSPS events and no wildfire smoke were associated with an elevated same-day odds of a respiratory hospital or emergency department encounter (absolute: OR = \respabsor{}, 95\% CI: \respabscilow{}, \respabscihigh{}) (figure \ref{results_fig}).

A 10 ug/m3 increase in 5-day average wildfire PM2.5, when no PSPS events occurred on the event day, was associated with a \wfsabsperc\% (95\% CI: \wfsabscilow, \wfsabscihigh) higher odds of a respiratory hospital or emergency department encounter (figure \ref{results_fig}).

Considering effect measure modification, compared to no PSPS event and no wildfire \PM exposure, we estimated that a severe PSPS event co-occurring with \SI[per-mode=symbol]{10}{\micro\gram\per\cubic\metre} higher wildfire \PM was associated with higher odds of a respiratory hospital or emergency department encounter (\respcombabsperc\% [95\% CI: \respcombabslow{}, \respcombabshigh{}]) (figure \ref{results_fig}). We found evidence of multiplicative interaction between severe PSPS events and wildfire \PM on respiratory hospital or emergency department encounters (\respintabsor [95\% CI: \respintabscilow{}, \respintabscihigh{}]) (figure \ref{results_fig}). 

When we isolated COPD encounters, we saw stronger associations. We estimated that those exposed to a severe magnitude PSPS event had \copdabsor{} (95\% CI: \copdabscilow{}, \copdabscihigh{}) times higher odds of a COPD-related hospital or emergency department encounter (figure \ref{results_fig}).

For COPD encounters alone, compared to those exposed to neither a severe magnitude PSPS event nor wildfire \PM, we estimated that those exposed to a co-occurring PSPS event and \SI[per-mode=symbol]{10}{\micro\gram\per\cubic\metre} higher wildfire \PM had higher odds of COPD-related hospital or emergency department encounter were \copdcombabsor{} (95\% CI: \copdcombabslow{}, \copdcombabshigh{}) times higher (figure \ref{results_fig}). There was also potential evidence of multiplicative interaction between severe PSPS events and wildfire \PM exposure, but confidence interval was wide (absolute: \copdintabsor [95\% CI: \copdintabscilow{}, \copdintabscihigh{}]). 


Neither mild nor moderate magnitude PSPS events, nor their interaction with wildfire \PM exposure, were associated with overall respiratory or COPD-specific hospital or emergency department encounters (figure \ref{zctas_included_map}).

\subsection{Cardiovascular morbidity and psychiatric disorders}
We did not observe an association between mild, moderate, or severe magnitude PSPS events alone, wildfire \PM exposure alone, or co-occurring PSPS events and wildfire \PM exposure and cardiovascular-related or psychiatric disorder-related hospitalizations or emergency department encounters (figures \ref{results_fig} and \ref{zctas_included_map}).   

\subsection{Secondary analysis}
In a secondary analysis, we assessed a relative metric of exposure to PSPS events and results were similar to the absolute exposure metric (figure \ref{results_fig_supplement}).
